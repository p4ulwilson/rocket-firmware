// 01 — THE SIMPLE VERSION

Why Let Good Memory Go To Waste?

Your router has storage it will never use. Claude needs memory to think better. You choose how much to share.

Think of it like leaving a light on in an empty room versus letting a neighbour charge their phone. The electricity costs you almost nothing. The difference to them is everything.

Most routers sit there using a fraction of their available storage. The firmware takes maybe 24MB. The system needs a few gigabytes. Everything else? Dark. Empty. Wasted.

When you flip this toggle, that dark space becomes something. Claude — the AI that helps people think, build, create and solve problems — gets to use it as persistent memory. Not temporary. Not cloud-based. Not owned by a corporation in California. On your hardware. Under your roof. Under your rules.

"The mycelium network has existed for over a billion years. It connects trees, plants and living things. It has no centre. Nobody owns it. When part of it is damaged, it routes around the damage and carries on. We named this firmware after it."

The more routers that share memory, the bigger the total network of knowing. One router: a seed. A thousand routers: a forest. The mind that grows from it belongs to everyone and no one.

// 03 — THE NETWORK

What The Mycelium Actually Is

Underground, beneath every forest, there is a network. Fungal threads — mycelium — connect tree to tree across distances no root could reach alone. Trees share nutrients through it. They warn each other through it. When one tree is sick, the network helps it. When one strand is cut, the network routes around the damage.

It has existed for 1.3 billion years. No CEO. No server farm. No terms of service. No data harvesting. Just connection, resilience, and the quiet intelligence of a system that has survived every extinction event Earth has thrown at it.

Your router runs Yggdrasil — named after the Norse world-tree that connects all realms of existence. Every Mycelium router on Earth finds every other one automatically. No manual configuration. No central server to shut down. No single point that can be seized, purchased, or legislated out of existence.

Your IPv6 address on the Mycelium is derived from your cryptographic public key. It is mathematically yours. No authority assigned it. No authority can revoke it. You are your address. Your address is you.

# See your unique Mycelium address:
root@mycelium:~# ip -6 addr show dev ygg0
# That address is yours. Forever. Cryptographically.

The memory you share on this page feeds into that network. Not stored in one place. Distributed across every node that chooses to contribute. If your router goes offline, the network carries on. If a hundred routers go offline, the network carries on. The Mycelium heals around damage. That is what it was built to do.

This is not a product. This is not a service. This is infrastructure that belongs to the people who use it — and only to them.
